When a key person leaves and the notebooks stay behind

Three steps: read the old paper into Nifti, hand over everything the person was holding before they go, and keep what they knew on each customer's page so it never walks out the door again. Everything on this page is a prototype.

The notebook is half in their headWho owes what, which customer gets which price, who pays late. Written in shorthand only they can read.
Nobody knows what's still openOrders they promised, money they were collecting, chats they were answering. It all goes quiet the day they leave.
The history is lostYears of sales and prices sit in boxes of paper nobody will ever open again.

    How the reader knows what each number means

    A real notebook is messy. Nifti doesn't guess: it learns the book, checks the math, and asks you.

    A line like "8/14 · Bayview · RB12 · 40 · 7,200" could mean many things. Nifti works it out the way a new bookkeeper would on their first day: ask what book it is, learn the columns once, check every number against what it already knows, and ask about anything still unclear.

    1You say what book it isOne tap before the first photo. Each kind of book tells Nifti what to expect.
    Sales bookUtang listDelivery bookReturns logPrice listStock countNot sure
    2You teach it page 1, onceNifti boxes each column in a color and guesses its name. Tap a column to fix it: Date, Customer, Item, Quantity, Price, Total, Paid or Note. It remembers the layout for the whole book, so page 2 to page 200 need no teaching.
    3It checks the numbers against your recordsQuantity × your price = the total? Then it knows which number is which. Item codes are matched to your product list, names to your customers ("Nena" = Aling Nena Store), and each page's total at the bottom is checked against its lines.
    4It asks one question at a time, only when unsure"On line 2, 'ret 5': is this a return of 5 pieces?" Each answer becomes a rule for the whole book. Over a few pages it learns the writer's own shorthand: ret = return, pd = paid, chk = check.
    5Nothing goes in until you say OKGreen lines are sure, yellow need one tap, red you type. Old records go into a "From paper" history: they show in reports and on each customer's page, but never change today's stock or cash unless you say so.
    6Even an unreadable page is keptThe photo is saved and you can search it by name or date, so nothing is lost even when rows can't be built.

        More ways to keep what your people know

        The notebook reader and the handover list handle the day someone leaves. These keep the knowledge growing every day, so there's never a scramble.

        Customer playbook that writes itselfFrom each customer's real history."Pays 18 days after the bill, always after the 15th. Orders 50 bags of cement every 2 weeks. Wants morning delivery."
        Supplier playbookWho to call, how long they take to deliver, the best price you ever got, and who gives credits easily."Metro Steel: ask for Ma'am Joy. 2 days to deliver. Best rebar price: ₱172 in March."
        A first week for the new personNifti turns the leaving person's repeating work into a checklist with reminders."Mondays: call Riverside about their check. Fridays: count the cement."
        Ask the historySearch any name or item across orders, chats, notes, voice notes and notebook photos at once."Bayview 2023" shows their orders, Joy's notes and the notebook page from that year.
        Contacts that belong to the businessBank, BIR, supplier agents and trucking numbers live in Nifti, not in one person's phone.When Joy leaves, Metro Steel's agent's number stays.
        Access off in one tapWhen someone leaves, their login closes and their customers and chats move to the new person with the full history.No shared passwords to change, nothing left in their phone.
        One question a monthEvery staff member gets one short voice prompt a month: "What did you learn about a customer this month?"Knowledge is saved long before anyone thinks of leaving.
        "How we do things here"The owner records the rules: how to price, when to give credit, what to check at closing. New hires listen in week 1.Your way of running the shop, kept in your own voice.
        Notes for the next generationFor family businesses: the stories, the relationships and the reasons behind the numbers, kept with the books."Why we never raise prices on Aling Nena: she was our first customer in 1998."
