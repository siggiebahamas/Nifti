Tax books: 3 simpler ways, with every total labeled

What's wrong today

Each invoice takes 7 rows (Invoice, Customer, Gross, Exempt, Vatable sales, VAT, Withheld). A busy month means hundreds of rows. The totals at the bottom have no labels, so nobody knows which number is which.

What the totals mean, in plain words:

  • Total sales (gross): everything on your invoices, VAT included.
  • VAT-exempt: sales with no VAT on them.
  • Sales before VAT (vatable): the part VAT is computed on.
  • VAT 12%: on sales, VAT you collected for the BIR; on purchases, VAT you already paid (you can subtract it).
  • Tax withheld (2307): tax a customer kept and paid to the BIR for you. You get it back as a credit.

All three options use the same sample month: 6 sales and 3 purchases.

1. One line per invoice

Like a normal list, BIR columns on request

Each invoice is one line with the 3 numbers people care about: amount, VAT and tax withheld. The 2 extra BIR columns (exempt, before-VAT) show when you tap "Show all BIR columns". Totals sit under their own labels.

2. Grouped by day

A day header, then one line per invoice

Reads like a diary: each day shows its total, then its invoices in one line each. The month totals are labeled boxes that say what each number means.

3. Your VAT first, details on tap

MY PICK

Starts with the answer: VAT you collected minus VAT you paid equals what you pay this month. Then 3 big labeled totals per side, and a short list. Tap an invoice to see all 7 numbers. Nothing is hidden, but nothing is in the way.
