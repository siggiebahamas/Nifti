Returns & credits: 3 calmer layouts

Why the page feels hard today

  • Two columns, each with 4 number boxes, a form, a list of credits and a list of bad orders. That's 10 blocks to read before you know what to do.
  • The forms are always open, even when you only came to check something.
  • Credit cards use labels like "Left" and "Status". People have to work out what they mean.

All three below use the same rules: what needs you comes first, forms stay closed until you ask for one, each credit is one plain sentence, and the blue and emerald sides keep their colors.

1. Pick who it's about first

One side at a time

Returns & credits

Money off for bad or returned items.

Who is this about?

Simplest to think about: the person only ever sees one side. Good if most of your credits go one way. The cost is one extra tap to see the other side.

2. One list, in plain sentences

MY PICK

Returns & credits

Money off for bad or returned items. You never do the math.

₱1,200comes off your customers' next bills
₱640comes off your next supplier bill
Snap their credit slip

    Reads like a to-do list: each line says who, how much, and what happens next. "Waiting" is open first, so the only action on the page, "Take it off now", is right there. Forms open only when you tap a button. On a phone it's one short column instead of today's very long page.
